The fourth bake on Bread Pitt was the first full run of the published [Light Whole Wheat Sourdough Sandwich Loaf](/recipes/light-whole-wheat-sourdough-sandwich-loaf/) recipe.

## Mixing and Building Strength

Bread Pitt was in a good mood that morning. The starter was active, bubbly, and ready to go, so the dough came together without any surprises.

{{ photo: starter-ready }}

After a short autolyse, the salt and olive oil went in, followed by about eight minutes of mixing. The dough came together into a smooth ball and headed into bulk fermentation.

{{ gallery: mixing }}

The first fold showed a little tearing, which isn't unusual with a dough that still has some whole wheat in it.

{{ gallery: first-fold }}

By the second fold the tearing had disappeared.

{{ gallery: second-fold }}

By the third fold the dough had developed the strength I was looking for. From there it was just a matter of waiting.

{{ gallery: third-fold }}

## Bulk Fermentation

Bulk fermentation took a little over seven hours. Using the new Cambro container made it easier to keep an eye on the dough, although I learned that a smaller, straighter-sided container would make judging the rise even easier in future bakes. The dough continued to expand steadily throughout the afternoon until it looked ready for shaping.

{{ gallery: bulk-fermentation }}

## The Overnight Proof

After shaping, the loaf wasn't going to finish proofing before bedtime. Rather than rushing it or baking late, I slid the pan into the refrigerator for an overnight proof. That's one of the nice things about sourdough—it can usually adapt to your schedule if you're willing to be patient.

{{ gallery: proof }}

The next morning, after about two hours back at room temperature, the loaf looked ready. Into the oven it went.

## The Results

It rewarded the patience with one of my better sandwich loaves so far. The oven spring was excellent, the crust baked to a rich golden brown, and the crumb was exactly what I wanted from a daily sandwich bread: soft, even, and sturdy enough to hold together while still feeling light.

{{ photo: hero }}

A few larger holes appeared near the top of the loaf, but overall the crumb was uniform and sliced beautifully.

{{ photo: crumb }}

This bake also reinforced something I've been learning over the past few months. Times printed in recipes are useful starting points, but the dough always gets the final vote. Watching how it develops, rather than chasing the clock, consistently produces better bread.

## Bread Pitt Rating

👍👍👍👍👍👍👍👍👍

**9/10 thumbs up**

The loaf turned out exactly the way I hoped. The only thing keeping it from a perfect score is that I'd like to refine the shaping a little more to eliminate the occasional larger air pocket near the top. Otherwise, this is a recipe I'll happily bake again.
